We sell cleaning business software, so treat this page with appropriate suspicion. We'll try to
earn it back by being straight with you: **if you're a solo cleaner with eight regular clients, a
spreadsheet is fine.** Better than fine. It's free, you already understand it, and it does not
send you onboarding emails.

Most articles on this topic exist to tell you that spreadsheets are chaos and you need software
today. That isn't true, and you'd know it wasn't.

What is true is that there's a specific point where a spreadsheet stops being the cheap option
and starts being the expensive one. This page is about finding that point.

## Where a spreadsheet genuinely works

- **A predictable, recurring book.** The same houses on the same days. You know the schedule
  because it's the same every week.
- **One person.** You are the calendar. Nobody else needs to see it.
- **Simple, stable pricing.** Flat rate per house, rarely quoted fresh.
- **Under roughly fifteen clients.** Small enough to hold in your head, with the sheet as backup.

If that's you, close this tab and go do something better with the twenty minutes.

## The four points where it stops working

Not "when you get big." These are the specific breakages, and they arrive in this order.

### 1. Your first employee

This is the big one, and it arrives sooner than people expect.

A spreadsheet is a record of what *you* know. The moment someone else needs to know it, the sheet
has to become a system of record — and it isn't one. You start texting screenshots. Someone works
from Tuesday's version on Thursday. Two people edit at once and one overwrites the other.

The failure isn't scheduling. It's that you now spend your evenings being a human API between the
sheet and your crew.

### 2. The first payment dispute

A client says the invoice is wrong, or that you didn't come on the 14th, or that the bathroom was
skipped.

Your spreadsheet says you were there. It does not say what time you arrived, how long you stayed,
or what the bathroom looked like when you left. You either eat it or you argue about it, and both
cost more than the job.

This is the one that surprises people. It isn't an efficiency problem — it's a revenue problem,
and one bad dispute a quarter can outweigh the entire cost of software.

### 3. Getting paid late, repeatedly

A spreadsheet tracks who owes you. It doesn't chase them.

You send an invoice, the client means to pay it, three weeks pass. You feel awkward following up,
so you don't, and now you're financing your client's cash flow.

When the invoice has a **Pay Now** button and a card or ACH option, most of that goes away — not
because clients become better people, but because paying became easier than remembering.

### 4. You stop being able to answer simple questions

"What did I make in April?" "Which clients are actually worth keeping?" "How much do I owe
Marcus for the last two weeks?"

Every one is answerable from a spreadsheet, and every one takes forty minutes. So you stop
asking, and you run the business on a feeling instead.

## The honest arithmetic

A spreadsheet costs $0 and some hours. Software costs money and some hours.

The comparison worth doing is against **your actual hourly rate**. If a Sunday evening of
scheduling, invoicing and chasing takes three hours, and you'd otherwise bill $50 an hour, that
evening costs you $150. Monthly, $600.

Set against $29.99, the question stops being whether software is worth it and becomes whether it
actually saves you those three hours. Hold any tool — ours included — to that.

## If you're not ready, take the spreadsheet seriously

Genuinely useful, and free:

- **One sheet, not five.** A tab for clients, a tab for the schedule, a tab for money. Not a
  workbook per month.
- **Put it in the cloud.** Google Sheets over a file on the laptop. The laptop will die.
- **Date everything.** A row without a date is a row you can't defend later.
- **Photograph your work anyway.** Even if the photos live in a phone album rather than attached
  to a job, having them beats not having them.
- **Send invoices the day of the clean**, not at month end. This single change does more for cash
  flow than any software will.
