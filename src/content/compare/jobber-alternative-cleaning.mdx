Jobber runs field service businesses: plumbers, HVAC techs, electricians, landscapers. It does
that well, and it has done it for over a decade.

But walk through a Jobber quote as a house cleaner and you feel the mismatch immediately. There
are fields for parts and materials. There's job costing built around labor hours against
equipment. The scheduling assumes a technician driving to a diagnosis, then a callback, then a
repair.

A recurring biweekly clean isn't shaped like that. You quote a whole-home price, not a parts
list. The same three houses come around every other Tuesday forever. The thing your client wants
proof of isn't a replaced capacitor. It's that the baseboards actually got done.

CleanDock is built for that job specifically. Here's an honest comparison so you can decide.

### If you're solo, this is close to a wash

Year one, prepaid, Jobber costs $252 and CleanDock costs $360. They're cheaper by about nine
dollars a month.

Year two, Jobber is $348 against CleanDock's $360. Twelve dollars apart over a full year.

We're not going to pretend that's a reason to switch. If you're a solo cleaner and happy to pay
twelve months up front, price shouldn't decide this. Pick on whether the tool matches how you
actually work, which is the rest of this page.

Two things to weigh that aren't in the sticker price: Jobber's rate is a prepaid annual
commitment, which is real money out of a solo operator's cash flow in January. And the
promotional rate lasts twelve months. CleanDock is month-to-month at $29.99, and stays $29.99.

One more thing worth saying plainly: Jobber is a bigger product, built for a wider set of trades.

## What a cleaning business actually needs

### Recurring cleans that repeat themselves

Most cleaning revenue is the same houses on a repeating cycle. Weekly, biweekly, monthly,
first-and-third-Thursday.

In CleanDock you set the recurrence once and the calendar fills itself out forward. You're not
rebuilding next month's schedule on a Sunday night, and you're not copying last week's jobs
across.

This is the single feature that decides whether software fits a cleaning business, which is why
it's the first thing we built rather than a setting inside a general scheduler.

### Before-and-after photos on every job

Cleaners get "you missed a room" more than any other complaint, and it's almost always a memory
problem rather than a work problem.

Your cleaners snap before and after photos from their phone at the job. The photos attach to
that job, permanently, alongside the tracked time. When a client questions it two weeks later,
you send the pictures instead of relitigating it.

For Airbnb turnovers and move-outs, where a deposit or a guest review is on the line, this stops
being a nice extra and becomes the reason you get rebooked.

## Where Jobber is genuinely the better choice

We'd rather you picked correctly than picked us.

**Choose Jobber if:**

- You run mixed trades: cleaning plus handyman, restoration, or property maintenance. Jobber's
  job costing is built around parts and materials.
- You need deep equipment or inventory tracking.
- You have a large office team and want a mature app marketplace and integration ecosystem.
- You want phone support with dedicated onboarding sessions included.

**Choose CleanDock if:**

- Cleaning is the business, not one service line among several.
- Recurring clients are most of your revenue.
- You want photo proof attached to every job without bolting on a second app.
- You're a crew of one to ten and don't want to pay per head to add your sister-in-law for the
  summer.
