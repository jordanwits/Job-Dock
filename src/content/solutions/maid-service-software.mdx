A residential maid service is a subscription business that nobody calls a subscription business.
The same houses, on the same cycle, more or less forever. Weekly, biweekly, monthly, first and
third Thursday.

Which means the single most valuable thing software can do for you is stop making you rebuild the
schedule.

## Recurring cleans that repeat themselves

Set the recurrence once. The calendar fills itself forward.

- Weekly, biweekly, monthly, or a custom pattern
- Every cleaner's week visible at a glance, so nobody gets double-booked
- Drag to reschedule when a client pushes a week

The Sunday-evening ritual of copying last week into next week stops. That's usually the hour
people notice first.

## Every client's history in one place

Contact details, the properties you clean, what you charge, and every job you've done for them —
with the photos and tracked time attached.

When a client calls asking what you charged last spring, or whether you did the oven in March, the
answer is in front of you rather than in a text thread you'd have to scroll.

## Clients who book themselves

One link on your website, your Instagram bio, or your Google Business Profile. New enquiries
arrive around the clock.

The request comes to you for approval first — you're not waking up to a job on the far side of
town at a price you wouldn't have quoted. Approve it and it's scheduled.

## Quote, invoice, paid

Most of your work is recurring, but the one-offs are where the margin is: deep cleans, spring
cleans, the move-out a client's daughter needs.

Build a branded quote from the driveway. They approve. One tap turns it into an invoice, and they
pay by card or ACH from the email. No re-keying line items, no waiting three weeks for a bank
transfer.

## Proof on every clean

Cleaners shoot before and after photos on their phone and clock their time. Both attach to the
job, permanently.

"You missed a room" is the most common complaint in residential cleaning and it's almost always a
memory problem. Photos end that conversation in one message.

## Paying your cleaners

Time tracked on the job rolls into an employee-pay report you can run by date range and hand to
your bookkeeper as a CSV. You aren't reconstructing hours from memory and text messages at the end
of the fortnight.
