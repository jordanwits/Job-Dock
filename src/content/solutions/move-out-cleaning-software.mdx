Move-out cleaning is a different business from recurring residential, even when the same crew does
both.

The jobs are one-offs. The tickets are bigger. The client is often a landlord, a property manager,
or a realtor rather than the person who lives there — and there's usually a deposit in dispute
somewhere behind it.

Two things decide whether you win this work and keep it: how fast the quote goes out, and whether
you can prove what you left behind.

## The quote has to go out before they call someone else

Move-out work is scheduled against a lease end date, which means the person calling you is already
short on time and calling more than one cleaner.

Build a branded quote on your phone, from the property, with the line items you actually intend to
do. Send it before you're out of the driveway. When they say yes, one tap turns it into an invoice
— same line items, no re-entry — and they pay by card or ACH from the email.

The cleaner whose quote arrives first, itemised and looking professional, wins a lot of these on
speed alone.

## Photos are evidence, not decoration

A deposit dispute is a disagreement about the condition of a property on a specific day. You are
frequently the only neutral party who was actually in the room.

Your cleaners shoot before and after photos on their phone. Every photo attaches to that job,
permanently, alongside the tracked time and any notes. When a landlord and a tenant disagree six
weeks later, you produce a timestamped record instead of a recollection.

This is what turns a one-off move-out into a repeat property-manager relationship. Managers keep
the cleaner who makes their disputes go away.

## Repeat buyers, one-off jobs

Property managers and realtors don't book recurring cleans, but they do call repeatedly.

- Every property and every past job kept against the contact
- What you charged last time, so you're consistent
- Reports by date range, so you know which managers are actually worth the drive

## Pricing that reflects the work

Move-outs vary enormously — an empty studio and a four-bedroom with a filthy oven are not the same
job. Saved line items let you build a quote from parts you've priced before rather than guessing
each time, and the quote is itemised so a client can see exactly what they're paying for.
