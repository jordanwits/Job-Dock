**CleanDock is built for small commercial and janitorial crews: roughly one to fifteen people,
servicing offices, retail units, medical suites, or a handful of contracts on a fixed schedule.**

If that's your operation, here's what it does for you.

## Contracts are just recurring schedules

Commercial work is contractual: this office, these nights, this scope, this monthly rate.

Set the recurrence once and the calendar builds itself forward. Weekly, several nights a week, or
a custom pattern. When you win a new contract you set it up once, assign the crew, and stop
thinking about it.

- Every crew member's schedule visible at a glance
- Drag to reschedule around a client's closure or a holiday
- No rebuilding the month every month

## Hours are the whole cost base

In residential, labour is a big cost. In commercial it's essentially the only one, and small
errors compound across a crew.

Cleaners clock in and out on their phone at the site. Time attaches to the job. At the end of the
period, the employee-pay report gives you hours by person by date range, ready to run and
exportable as CSV for your bookkeeper.

You're not reconstructing a fortnight of hours from text messages.

## Proof for a client who isn't there

Nobody from the client's business is in the building when you clean it. That's the whole point,
and it's also why the relationship can quietly sour. The office manager only ever sees what was
missed.

Photos attached to each job give you something to send when a contract is up for renewal, or when
someone claims the kitchen hasn't been done in a fortnight.

## Invoicing a business, not a homeowner

Commercial clients pay on terms, by bank transfer more often than by card.

Invoices go out branded and itemised, with card or ACH both available. ACH matters here, because
a business paying a four-figure monthly invoice is not reaching for a credit card. QuickBooks sync
keeps it off your desk.
