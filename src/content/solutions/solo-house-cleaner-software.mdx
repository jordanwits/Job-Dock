Most cleaning software is priced and built for a company with a crew and someone answering the
phone. When you're the owner, the cleaner, the scheduler and the person chasing invoices, that
software is both too expensive and too complicated.

The usual result is a phone's notes app, a spreadsheet, and a lot of Sunday evenings.

Fourteen-day free trial, month to month, cancel whenever, and your data exports anytime.

## Your week builds itself

You almost certainly have a core of regulars: the same houses, the same rhythm.

Set the recurrence once, whether weekly, biweekly or monthly, and the calendar fills itself
forward.
That's the Sunday evening back.

## Get paid without the awkward text

This is the part solo cleaners lose most money to. The work is done, the invoice is somewhere, and
following up feels rude, so it slides.

Send a branded invoice from your phone the day of the clean. It has a Pay Now button for card or
ACH. Most people pay on the spot, not because they became more conscientious but because paying
became easier than remembering.

For new work, build a quote from the driveway. They approve, one tap turns it into an invoice.

## Look like a business

A branded quote and a proper invoice change how people treat your prices. So does a booking link
you can put in your Instagram bio or your Google Business Profile instead of "just text me."

You're the same cleaner doing the same work. You just stop being negotiated with quite so often.

## Photos that protect you

Snap before and after photos on your phone. They attach to the job permanently, alongside your
tracked time.

When someone says the bathroom wasn't done, you send the picture. As a solo operator you don't
have a manager to back you up. The photos are that.

## Everything on the phone

Quote from the driveway, clock in at the door, photograph on the way out, invoice from the car.
There's a desktop view when you want it, but you don't need one.
