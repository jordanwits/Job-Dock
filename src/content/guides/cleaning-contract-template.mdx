Most residential cleaning starts on a handshake and a text message. That is fine until the first
disagreement, at which point it turns out nobody wrote down what "clean" meant, what happens on a
holiday, or how much notice cancelling requires.

A one page agreement solves nearly all of it. Below is a template you can copy, followed by an
explanation of why each clause exists.

## The template

Copy everything between the lines and replace the bracketed parts.

---

**CLEANING SERVICE AGREEMENT**

This agreement is between **[Your Business Name]** ("Provider") and **[Client Name]** ("Client"),
effective **[Date]**.

**1. Services**

Provider will clean the property at **[Address]** on a **[weekly / biweekly / monthly]** schedule,
on **[day of week]**, beginning **[start date]**.

The scope of each visit is listed in Schedule A below. Work outside that scope is quoted
separately and must be agreed in writing before it is performed.

**2. Price and payment**

The fee is **$[amount]** per visit. First or deep cleans are billed at **$[amount]**.

Invoices are issued on the day of service and due within **[7 / 14]** days. Late balances may
incur a fee of **[amount or percentage]** after **[number]** days.

**3. Access**

Client will provide access by **[key / lockbox code / smart lock / being present]**. If Provider
cannot access the property at the scheduled time, Client is charged **[full / 50 percent]** of the
visit fee.

**4. Cancellation and rescheduling**

Either party may cancel or reschedule a visit with at least **[24 / 48]** hours notice. Visits
cancelled with less notice are charged at **[full / 50 percent]** of the visit fee.

Either party may end this agreement with **[14 / 30]** days written notice.

**5. Scheduled closures**

Provider does not attend on **[list holidays]**. Affected visits are rescheduled to the nearest
available date or skipped by agreement.

**6. Client responsibilities**

Client will secure pets, valuables and confidential documents before each visit, and will notify
Provider of hazards, broken fixtures or areas to avoid.

**7. Supplies and equipment**

Provider supplies all cleaning products and equipment unless otherwise agreed. If Client requires
specific products, Client supplies them and accepts responsibility for their suitability.

**8. Damage and liability**

Provider carries liability insurance and will report any damage caused by Provider within 24
hours. Claims must be reported by Client within **[48 / 72]** hours of the visit.

Provider is not liable for damage arising from pre-existing wear, faulty fixtures, or items not
disclosed under clause 6.

**9. Satisfaction**

If Client is dissatisfied with any area, Client will notify Provider within **[24 / 48]** hours
and Provider will return to re-clean that area at no charge. Refunds are at Provider's discretion.

**10. Non-solicitation**

Client agrees not to directly employ Provider's staff for cleaning services during this agreement
or for **[6 / 12]** months after it ends.

**11. Photographs**

Provider may take before and after photographs of work areas for quality and record purposes.
Photographs are not used publicly without Client's written consent.

**12. Entire agreement**

This document is the entire agreement between the parties and replaces any prior discussion. It
may only be changed in writing signed by both parties.

**SCHEDULE A: SCOPE OF WORK**

*Every visit:*
[e.g. All rooms dusted, floors vacuumed and mopped, kitchen surfaces and exterior of appliances,
bathrooms cleaned and sanitised, bins emptied, beds made]

*Not included:*
[e.g. Interior of oven or refrigerator, windows, laundry, dishes, tidying of personal items,
areas above standing height]

Provider: ______________________ Date: __________

Client: ________________________ Date: __________

## Why each clause is there

**Scope, and the "not included" list.** This is the clause that prevents most disputes. Scope
creep in residential cleaning is almost never malicious, it is a client assuming the oven was
always part of it. Writing down what is excluded is more valuable than writing down what is
included.

**Access and lockout fees.** You drove there. Your cleaner's time is spent. Without this clause
you eat that cost, and it happens more than new owners expect.

**Cancellation notice.** A last minute cancellation is not a free day off, it is a gap in a
scheduled route you cannot fill at short notice. Charging for it is standard and clients accept
it when it was agreed up front.

**The 24 to 48 hour complaint window.** Without a window, you get called about a clean from three
weeks ago. With one, the conversation happens while the facts are fresh and re-cleaning is still
practical.

**Non-solicitation.** Clients hiring your cleaner directly is one of the more common ways cleaning
businesses lose both an employee and an account at once. A clause does not make it impossible, but
it makes it a deliberate act rather than a casual one.

**The photography clause.** Before and after photos are the single best protection you have in a
disagreement, and clients are far more comfortable with them when consent was established in
advance rather than requested mid dispute.

## Getting it signed without friction

The agreement only works if it is signed, and long documents sent as attachments get postponed
indefinitely.

Two things help. Keep it to one page plus the scope schedule, and send it at the same time as the
quote, while the client is already deciding. A quote and an agreement arriving together reads as
professional. An agreement arriving a week later reads as a complication.
